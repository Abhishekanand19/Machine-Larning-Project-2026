# Viva questions for this implementation

1. **What is formation flying?** Aircraft maintain a relative arrangement while flying. Our experiment estimates the leader's pose relative to a follower camera.

2. **What is pose, and which components do you estimate?** Pose describes position and orientation. We estimate X, Y, Z, and roll; pitch and yaw are fixed to zero.

3. **Why did you generate synthetic data?** It gives reproducible observations with exact labels without aircraft, camera collection, or manual annotation. It limits claims to this simulated domain.

4. **Why exactly 14 keypoints and 42 inputs?** The reference paper uses 14 structural features. Each identified point supplies two image coordinates and one visibility flag, giving 14 × 3 = 42 inputs.

5. **What are the aircraft points?** Nose, four fuselage/tail-axis points, three points on each wing, two lateral stabilizers, and one vertical-tail point. Their body-frame coordinates and order are fixed in `src/utils.py`.

6. **What is occlusion in this project?** A feature is missing from the observation. We approximate occlusion or missed detections with independent 10% random dropping and also reject off-frame points. We do not ray-trace physical self-occlusion.

7. **How does your camera projection work?** Forward depth is Xc. We calculate u = cx + fx·Yc/Xc and v = cy − fy·Zc/Xc, after roll rotation and translation. Nonpositive depth is invalid.

8. **Why normalize inputs and scale targets?** Normalizing pixels to [-1,1] keeps inputs at a stable magnitude. Training-only StandardScaler balances metres and degrees in the loss. Visibility stays binary, and inference inverse-transforms the four outputs.

9. **How are invisible points encoded?** Their input triple is exactly (0,0,0). A visible point at the image center can have zero coordinates but flag 1, so it remains distinguishable.

10. **What is the train/validation/test split and why is the test set unseen?** 14,000/3,000/3,000 observations, generated by a seed-42 permutation. Training updates weights, validation selects the checkpoint, and the test set measures performance after training. Using test labels to tune the model would bias that evaluation.

11. **Why use an ANN rather than a CNN?** We already have identified structural coordinates. A compact 42–100–100–4 feed-forward network models their nonlinear relationship to pose. A CNN would require images and a separate detection task.

12. **Why ReLU, Adam, and MSE?** ReLU gives the network nonlinearity with simple computation. Adam adjusts updates using gradient history. Squared differences between standardized predicted and true targets provide a straightforward regression objective.

13. **Why regression instead of the reference paper's classification?** Our outputs are four continuous quantities, so regression is easier to explain and avoids discretization into 4,800 classes. We do not claim reproduction of the paper's full system or particle filter.

14. **What is overfitting, and what do the loss curves show?** Overfitting means fitting training details while generalization worsens. Falling training loss with persistently rising validation loss is a warning. We use a validation-selected checkpoint and early-stopping patience of 40; the executed best epoch was 299 of 300.

15. **How do you know the model learned rather than predicting the average?** Loss dropped substantially; the test scatter plots follow the diagonal; correlations exceed 0.99 for all outputs; different observations produce different predictions with standard deviations close to those of true poses.

16. **What do the demo errors mean?** 3D position error is Euclidean distance between predicted and true position for one sample. Roll error is the absolute angular difference for that sample. They are not a guarantee of performance on other samples.

17. **What are your limitations and future scope?** Fixed geometry, known point identities, calibrated camera, zero pitch/yaw, and simple synthetic noise. We have no real detector, uncertainty, filtering, or controller. Future work could evaluate new cameras/geometries, systematic occlusion, pitch/yaw, and labelled real keypoints.

18. **Can a single camera estimate depth?** With a known aircraft size, apparent feature scale provides depth information. Unknown size creates scale ambiguity. Our depth estimate is tied to the fixed known geometry, and noise is more influential when the aircraft appears small.
